Genre

Shoegaze

A subgenre of alternative rock defined by layers of distorted, effects-laden guitars and softly buried vocals — named for performers who stared down at their effects pedals on stage.
Shoegaze emerged in the United Kingdom in the late 1980s, crystallized by a generation of bands obsessed with the textural possibilities of the electric guitar. Where earlier post-punk sought clarity and edge, shoegaze sought immersion: walls of detuned chords, vibrato bars worked in real time, and vocals mixed flush with the instrumentation rather than above it. The genre's defining gestures — Kevin Shields' "glide guitar," the wash of reverb and tremolo, the indifference to traditional song structure — were a deliberate inversion of rock-star theatrics. The performers literally looked down at their pedalboards, hence the journalistic shorthand that stuck.

Node · Label (focused)

Label

~scape

Stefan Betke's Berlin imprint, a hub for the post-techno minimalism that filtered dub through laptops at the turn of the millennium.
Founded in 2000 by Stefan Betke — better known as Pole — ~scape became the connective tissue between Germany's post-techno avant-garde and the international laptop-music scene. Where parent label Kompakt foregrounded melody and pop sensibility, ~scape pursued texture, weight, and the long decay tails of dub. The roster — Jan Jelinek, Bus, Burnt Friedman, Andrew Pekler — shared an obsession with sampled-but-dissolved source material: jazz records reduced to their grain, dub plates abstracted into pulse.

Node · Artist (focused)

Artist

Jan Jelinek

Berlin producer whose grain-fine sampling of jazz records redefined what a beat could be made of.
Jan Jelinek emerged from the Frankfurt techno scene in the late 1990s but found his signature voice in Berlin, where 2001's Loop-finding-jazz-records dissolved the entire jazz canon into a series of softly clicking, breath-textured loops. The record became a touchstone for an entire micro-generation of producers learning to hear the surface noise of an LP as a compositional element in its own right. Across subsequent releases — under his own name and as Farben, Gramm, and Ursula Bogner — Jelinek has continued to interrogate the boundary between sampling and abstraction, treating found sound less as material than as atmosphere.

Node · Project (focused)

Project

Loop-finding-jazz-records

The record that taught a generation of producers to hear the dust of an LP as composition.
Released on Stefan Betke's ~scape in 2001, Loop-finding-jazz-records is built almost entirely from short loops cut out of obscure 1960s and 70s jazz records — the kind of three-second fragments most producers would discard for being too inert. Jelinek heard otherwise: by stripping out the recognizable hooks and keeping only the surface noise and the harmonic shadow, he produced a record that feels less sampled than dreamed.

Tendency

The closing track of Loop-finding-jazz-records — a single dissolving loop stretched into a quiet manifesto.
"Tendency" closes the record with one of Jelinek's purest gestures: a brief lift from an unnamed jazz LP, slowed almost to stasis, looped until its harmonic content becomes a kind of breathing. Where the record's earlier tracks build small architectures around their loops, "Tendency" lets the source breathe alone.
